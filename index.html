Robotics · Learning · Control

Jordan H. Hsieh

I combine physical models with data to help robots fly and operate reliably when their dynamics change.

At Penn’s ScalAR Lab, I studied how nearby walls affect quadrotor flight and tested whether models learned from short flights could improve control. I’m interested in how these methods transfer to unfamiliar environments and robot teams.

Featured Project

University of Pennsylvania · Robotics

Quadrotor flight through narrow tubes

I combined reduced-order aerodynamic models with learned residual dynamics to predict confinement effects. The approach learned from less than 30 seconds of flight data and reduced tracking error by up to 76% on hardware relative to a nominal controller.

First-author manuscript in preparation for IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters.

About

I earned my M.S.E. in Aerospace Engineering at the University of Michigan. Earlier, I worked on electric propulsion and energy systems at National Cheng Kung University and co-founded Tensor Tech, where I developed satellite attitude control systems. At Penn, I served as a teaching assistant for Mathematics for Robotics and Advanced Robotics. I hope to pursue a faculty career that combines research with mentoring future engineers.
