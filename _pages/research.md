## Reliable autonomy under uncertain dynamics

I study how robots can predict and adapt to physical interactions that are difficult to model completely. My interests include physics-informed learning, robust model predictive control, and multi-robot coordination under partial observability.

### Confined-space quadrotor flight

At the University of Pennsylvania, I developed and evaluated adaptive control methods for quadrotors flying through narrow tubes. The work combines a physics-based model of wall effects with a learned neural ODE correction inside MPC, then tests the controller on Crazyflie hardware with motion capture.

### Multi-robot coordination

I am exploring whether learned models of physical interactions can be composed to predict the behavior of new robot teams. Cooperative aerial payload transport is a useful setting for studying unfamiliar team sizes, teammate dynamics, and limited communication.

### Earlier work in aerospace systems

At National Cheng Kung University, I worked on Hall thrusters, hollow cathodes, plasma diagnostics, and Gaussian-process surrogate models for nonlinear plasma behavior. At Tensor Tech, I worked on satellite attitude-control architectures, actuator systems, and in-orbit simulation tools.
