I am **Jordan H. Hsieh**, a researcher working at the intersection of robotics, learning-based control, and aerospace systems. My research asks how autonomous systems can remain reliable when their dynamics change, measurements are limited, and learned models face conditions outside their training data.

At the University of Pennsylvania's GRASP Lab, I worked on quadrotor flight through confined spaces, combining a physics-based aerodynamic model with a neural correction inside model predictive control. Earlier, I developed Gaussian-process surrogate models for plasma propulsion systems and worked on satellite attitude determination and control at Tensor Tech. I earned an M.S. in Aerospace Engineering from the University of Michigan and a B.S. in Mechanical Engineering from National Cheng Kung University.

## Research interests

- Physics-informed system identification and learning-based control
- Robust autonomy under model mismatch and domain shift
- Multi-robot coordination with limited sensing and communication
- Aerial robotics, spacecraft control, and electric propulsion

I am interested in composing learned physical interaction models to predict unseen multi-robot configurations, with cooperative aerial payload transport as a motivating testbed.
