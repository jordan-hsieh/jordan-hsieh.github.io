Research

I work on dynamics modeling and control with limited measurements. These projects span aerial robotics, propulsion, spacecraft control, and distributed energy systems.

University of Pennsylvania · Robotics

Quadrotor flight through narrow tubes

I combined reduced-order aerodynamic models with learned residual dynamics to predict confinement effects. The approach learned from less than 30 seconds of flight data and reduced tracking error by up to 76% on hardware relative to a nominal controller.

First-author manuscript in preparation for IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters.

I built a reconfigurable 12-meter testbed for horizontal and vertical flight. The hybrid model used a neural network to learn the aerodynamic effects the physical model missed. I used its predictions as feedforward compensation in a geometric controller and tested it across tube diameters.

National Cheng Kung University · Propulsion

Predicting plasma behavior from sparse data

I developed Gaussian process surrogate models to relate thruster operating conditions to plasma responses when detailed simulation was too costly. I also explored coupling models of individual thrusters and plume interactions to predict untested cluster configurations.

Plasma responds rapidly to changes in electrical inputs, but laboratory measurements reveal only part of what is happening inside the discharge. The surrogate models provided a tractable way to make predictions from those measurements. The cluster work explored how local plasma conditions could connect models of individual thrusters and their interactions.

Tensor Tech · Space systems

Satellite attitude control

I developed sensor fusion using an extended Kalman filter and co-designed control algorithms and actuator architectures for small satellites under torque and power constraints.

At Tensor Tech, I worked on attitude estimation and control together with the hardware that produces torque. I built simulation tools to evaluate pointing and power consumption in orbit and used a test bench to characterize the control system.

National Cheng Kung University · Energy systems

Coordinating retractable energy units

Each unit houses solar and wind generators underground and raises the better option as local weather changes. I led the development of local sensing, a machine-learning weather model, and coordinated deployment decisions. The system increased energy generation by 47% over a baseline using regional forecasts alone.

U.S. Patent 12,308,643 B2

Regional forecasts were too coarse to decide which generator each unit should raise. We used a local sensor network and a weather model to estimate conditions at each unit, then coordinated those decisions across the network.
